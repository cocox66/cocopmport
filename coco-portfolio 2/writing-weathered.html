Weathered

creative nonfiction · Coco Xia

I wandered around in Maggie Nelson’s Bluets. It was like a maze, its scatteredness pulling me into an intricately woven network of befuddled thoughts and untold stories. Suppose I also fell in love with the color blue, as passionately as she did.

I realized that in my distant memories lay a particular shade of blue that fascinates me. It wasn’t quite like the blinding turquoise that made her life remarkable; it was a more gentle, velvety blend of sapphire and azure — the plumage of a cerulean warbler that would winter in the foothills of somewhere like Belize. In fact, it was the color of the waves in Pacuare Reserve, Costa Rica, a place that could pull me into tangled trains of thoughts every time I reminisced about the people I met, the dreams I had, the things I did and did not do. The ocean of Pacuare followed no rules. With each wave that crashed upon the dusky, sandy shore, the sea sang symphonies of sounds and echoed eternal rhythms of tides. The water unleashed its power at the beach, pouring, unrestrained, only to recede quietly into the sea, like a child’s apology to a careless wrongdoing, a lover’s tender retreat.
